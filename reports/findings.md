# Evidence-based findings

## Executed scope

These findings use only the 48-row NHS England Public Data Gateway verification snapshot covering England plus Northumbria Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust, Royal Berkshire NHS Foundation Trust and Mid And South Essex NHS Foundation Trust from August 2025 to July 2026, plus the 14-row selected-provider July 2026 benchmark extract. They do **not** pretend the restricted-runtime build has executed the full provider/specialty RTT warehouse.

## National direction

- The published England proportion waiting within 18 weeks increased from **61.0% in August 2025 to 65.4% in July 2026**, a **+4.4 percentage-point** change.
- The published England proportion waiting over 52 weeks decreased from **2.6% to 1.5%** over the same period, a **-1.1 percentage-point** change.
- NHS England’s 10 September 2026 operational publication reported the total waiting list increased by **55,961 to 7.3 million in July 2026**, with **65.4% waiting up to 18 weeks**. This confirms that improvement in the percentage metric can coexist with month-to-month growth in the absolute waiting list; stock and timeliness must therefore be analysed together.

## Provider contrast in the verified 12-month sample

- **Northumbria Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust:** within-18-week performance increased from **82.0% to 83.9%** (+1.9pp). Its tabular over-52-week series remained **0.0%** throughout the 12 months. In July 2026 its 18-week proportion was **18.5pp above** the national average.
- **Royal Berkshire NHS Foundation Trust:** within-18-week performance increased from **79.7% to 83.3%** (+3.6pp), while the over-52-week series remained **0.1%**. In July 2026 the 18-week proportion was **17.9pp above** the national average.
- **Mid And South Essex NHS Foundation Trust:** within-18-week performance moved from **50.6% to 49.8%** (-0.8pp). Its over-52-week proportion moved from **7.8% to 7.9%** (+0.1pp) after reaching 7.0% in March-April 2026. In July it was **15.6pp below** the national 18-week average and **6.4pp above** the national over-52-week proportion. This makes it a defensible candidate for deeper provider/specialty/flow investigation, not a causal judgement about care quality.

## Selected-provider July 2026 screening

The selected benchmark extract deliberately includes two strong comparator providers and twelve lower-18-week providers visible in the official gateway. It is **not a representative sample**, so no prevalence/generalisation is made from sample counts. Among the selected rows, Mid And South Essex had the lowest observed within-18-week percentage (49.8%) and highest observed over-52-week percentage (7.9%). Hull (56.0% / 3.9%), University Hospitals Coventry and Warwickshire (58.3% / 3.7%) and East Kent (55.2% / 3.2%) also combine a below-national 18-week proportion with an above-national >52-week proportion in July 2026 and are therefore reasonable screening candidates for full-source analysis.

## What is deliberately not claimed yet

No provider backlog-growth percentage, demand-throughput ratio, specialty driver, inequality gap, bed-capacity relationship, composite pressure score or forecast is published as a finding until the corresponding official bulk files are successfully ingested and pass reconciliation. The repository contains the code/model to calculate them, but not fabricated outputs.
